# Game Animation & Development Studio: Academic Year Course Outline

## Planning Assumptions

- The academic year has three 10-week terms.
- Wednesday lunchtime sessions are 30 minutes or less.
- Google Classroom carries the main lessons, tutorials, starter files, and help resources.
- Wednesday sessions are for WIP review, live user experience, feedback, community, and next-step direction.
- Godot 2D is the core engine for the shared pathway.
- Students may enter at different Studio Levels, but the year should still have a shared rhythm.

## Core Teaching Principle

The initial pathway uses a find-it/fix-it model.

Students should begin by working with small existing projects, broken demos, or incomplete prototypes. Their first task is not to build a full game from scratch. Their first task is to:

1. Find what is unclear, missing, unfair, broken, or weak.
2. Fix or improve one thing.
3. Test the change with another person.
4. Explain how the player experience changed.

This keeps early work concrete, visible, and manageable.

## Year Goal

By the end of the year, students should be able to move from guided repair and modification into original design and showcase-ready work.

```text
Find problems -> fix small systems -> test with users -> explain impact -> design original improvements -> showcase work
```

## Studio Level Fit

| Studio Level | Year-End Target |
|---|---|
| Explorer | Can modify and explain a simple 2D game prototype. |
| Builder | Can repair, tune, and extend a playable arcade-style game. |
| Designer | Can improve story, UI, feedback, systems, and user experience. |
| Developer | Can contribute to a team project with a defined production role. |
| Director | Can prepare a polished demo, pitch, and documentation for showcase. |

# Term 1: Find It, Fix It, Play It

## Term Focus

Students learn the studio workflow through small repairs and modifications to existing Godot 2D starter projects.

The goal is fast playable wins, not full original production.

## Term 1 Core Question

What changed in the player's experience when I changed the game?

## Term 1 Outputs

By the end of Term 1, each student or team should have:

- A modified Godot 2D starter project.
- Evidence of at least three small fixes or improvements.
- One user feedback note.
- A short reflection explaining how one change affected the player experience.

## Term 1 Weekly Outline

| Week | Google Classroom Focus | Starter/Fix-It Task | Wednesday Session |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Studio orientation, Google Classroom setup, laptop/group readiness | No build task. Complete interest/readiness form and join Classroom. | Orientation And Placement Meeting |
| 2 | What makes something a game? Goals, rules, feedback, results | Play a tiny starter game and identify what is clear or unclear. | First WIP Check-In |
| 3 | Godot basics: open, run, save, find the project folder | Fix project organization: correct folder, naming, saving, and reopening. | Problem Triage Meeting |
| 4 | Movement and controls | Fix player movement: too slow, too fast, slippery, or hard to control. | Wednesday WIP Review |
| 5 | Collectibles, score, and visible feedback | Fix weak feedback: collectible disappears, score updates, message appears, or sound plays. | Critique By Design Lens: Feedback |
| 6 | Hazards, boundaries, and fairness | Fix unfair challenge: hazard placement, speed, damage, lives, or boundaries. | Wednesday WIP Review |
| 7 | UI and player instructions | Fix unclear UI: title, controls, score, objective, win/lose text. | Critique By Design Lens: Readability |
| 8 | User testing: observe before explaining | Watch another student play and record where they hesitate or misunderstand. | Wednesday WIP Review |
| 9 | Revision from user feedback | Choose one user problem and improve it. | WIP Review / Problem Triage |
| 10 | Mini showcase: before and after | Present one fix and explain how it changed the experience. | Showcase And Celebration |

## Recommended Term 1 Starter Projects

1. **Broken Collector**
   - Player moves.
   - Collectibles exist.
   - Score or feedback is weak or missing.
   - Hazards may be unfair.

2. **Movement Playground**
   - Player movement works but needs tuning.
   - Students adjust speed, boundaries, collision, and feel.

3. **Unclear UI Demo**
   - Game works mechanically but the player does not know what to do.
   - Students improve instructions, labels, and feedback.

## Term 1 Assessment Lens

Students are not assessed on polish. They are assessed on whether they can:

- Run and save a project.
- Identify a problem.
- Make a small change.
- Test the change.
- Explain the effect on the player.

# Term 2: Fix It, Extend It, Design It

## Term Focus

Students move from repairing existing demos to extending them with small original features, clearer systems, and more intentional design.

## Term 2 Core Question

How can I extend a working prototype without making it too large or confusing?

## Term 2 Outputs

By the end of Term 2, each student or team should have:

- An extended playable prototype.
- A simple concept sheet or mini game design document.
- Evidence of at least two user feedback cycles.
- A short list of design decisions and why they were made.

## Term 2 Weekly Outline

| Week | Google Classroom Focus | Starter/Fix-It/Extend Task | Wednesday Session |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Review Term 1 and choose a project direction | Select one starter project to extend or one approved original idea to keep small. | Planning And Team Formation |
| 2 | Concept clarity: goal, player action, audience | Fix the concept: write the goal, main action, and what the player should feel. | Critique By Design Lens: Concept Clarity |
| 3 | Level design and player path | Extend the level: add a second area, route, obstacle, or objective. | Wednesday WIP Review |
| 4 | UI/UX and learnability | Improve instructions, prompts, menus, and feedback based on a new user. | Critique By Design Lens: User Experience |
| 5 | Story, setting, and theme in games | Add or improve context: character, setting, objective, choice, or consequence. | Wednesday WIP Review |
| 6 | Game systems: variables, timers, lives, inventory, quest state | Add one simple system without breaking the main loop. | Problem Triage Meeting |
| 7 | Animation and sound as feedback | Add one animation or sound cue that tells the player something useful. | Critique By Design Lens: Animation/Feedback |
| 8 | User testing round | Let someone else try the game with minimal explanation. | Wednesday WIP Review |
| 9 | Revision sprint | Fix the biggest user experience problem found in testing. | WIP Review / Problem Triage |
| 10 | Mid-year showcase | Demonstrate the extended prototype and explain one important design decision. | Showcase And Celebration |

## Term 2 Project Options

Students may continue from a Term 1 starter project or move into one of these controlled project types:

- Timed collector.
- Dodger/survival game.
- Maze or route-planning game.
- Short narrative interaction.
- Quest/objective prototype.
- Team arcade prototype.

## Term 2 Assessment Lens

Students are assessed on whether they can:

- Keep a project in scope.
- Extend a working prototype.
- Use feedback to make a design decision.
- Explain mechanics, UI, story, or systems clearly.
- Show evidence of testing and revision.

# Term 3: Test It, Polish It, Showcase It

## Term Focus

Students prepare a project or artifact for public or semi-public presentation. The focus is polish, documentation, user testing, and clear explanation.

## Term 3 Core Question

What must be improved so another person can understand, play, and appreciate this work?

## Term 3 Outputs

By the end of Term 3, each student or team should have:

- A playable demo, interactive prototype, game-animation sample, or design artifact.
- A short project explanation.
- Asset credits.
- User feedback evidence.
- A reflection explaining what changed and why.
- Optional pitch or showcase presentation.

## Term 3 Weekly Outline

| Week | Google Classroom Focus | Test/Polish Task | Wednesday Session |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scope check: what can realistically be finished? | Cut or park features that are too large. Define the showcase version. | Planning And Team Formation |
| 2 | Production checklist: must-have vs nice-to-have | Identify the minimum complete version. | Problem Triage Meeting |
| 3 | Playtest for clarity | Test whether a new user knows what to do. Fix the first confusion point. | Wednesday WIP Review |
| 4 | Playtest for fairness and challenge | Test difficulty, pacing, hazard placement, timing, or level flow. | Critique By Design Lens: Challenge |
| 5 | Visual polish and feedback | Improve readability, animation, sound, layout, or feedback. | WIP Review |
| 6 | Documentation and credits | Complete concept summary, tools used, assets, credits, and reflection notes. | Documentation Check |
| 7 | Pitch and explanation | Practice explaining the project in two minutes. | Critique By Design Lens: Presentation |
| 8 | Final user test | Run a near-final test and record one last improvement. | Wednesday WIP Review |
| 9 | Showcase rehearsal | Check demo readiness, order, laptop access, and speaking notes. | Showcase Preparation |
| 10 | Final showcase and reflection | Present finished or best-available work. | Showcase And Celebration |

## Term 3 Assessment Lens

Students are assessed on whether they can:

- Prepare work for another person to experience.
- Respond to user feedback.
- Explain what changed over time.
- Credit assets and tools appropriately.
- Present work respectfully and clearly.

# Weekly Google Classroom Post Template

Each weekly post should include:

1. **Focus:** the main idea for the week.
2. **Watch/Read:** short tutorial or concept note.
3. **Starter File:** project or file to use.
4. **Find It:** what problem students should look for.
5. **Fix It:** what they should change.
6. **Test It:** who should try it and what to observe.
7. **Explain It:** one or two reflection questions.
8. **Bring Wednesday:** screenshot, demo, question, or feedback note.

## Example Weekly Post

```text
Focus: Player movement and game feel

Find It:
Play the starter project. Is the player too slow, too fast, slippery, or hard to control?

Fix It:
Change one movement value. Test it again.

Test It:
Ask someone else to play for 30 seconds. Watch before explaining.

Explain It:
What did you change? How did it affect the player experience?

Bring Wednesday:
Be ready to show the before/after difference or describe what you discovered.
```

# Standing Rule For Early Work

For the first stage of the club:

> Do not start with a blank screen unless you already know what you are doing. Start with a small working project, find one problem, fix one thing, and explain the difference.
