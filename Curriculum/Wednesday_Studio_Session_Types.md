# Game Animation & Development Studio: Wednesday Session Types

## Purpose

Wednesday lunchtime sessions are short studio meetings, not full lessons or technical labs. The main teaching, tutorials, and concept development should happen through Google Classroom. Wednesday sessions create rhythm, accountability, community, feedback, and live user experience.

The normal session length is 30 minutes or less.

## Core Principle

Wednesday is where work meets people.

Students should come prepared to show progress, observe how others respond, receive feedback, and identify the next step. The session should not be treated as a place where every technical problem will be solved live.

## Session Type 1: Wednesday WIP Review

### When To Use

Use this as the default weekly meeting format once students have begun making work.

This is best when students or teams have prototypes, sketches, animations, screenshots, storyboards, level layouts, UI mockups, or playable tests to show.

### Focus

Work-in-progress review, live user experience, feedback, and next-step direction.

This meeting helps students learn that a design does not only exist in the creator's head. A game, animation, interface, level, or story idea must be experienced by someone else before the designer can understand how well it communicates.

### What Students Bring

- A playable prototype, screenshot, sketch, animation, storyboard, level layout, or design document.
- One question they want feedback on.
- Evidence of what changed since the last review.
- If showing a digital project, access to a laptop or a team member's laptop.

### 30-Minute Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-3 min | Arrival and setup. Demo students open their work. |
| 3-5 min | Weekly focus. Teacher names the review lens, such as clarity, controls, feedback, challenge, story, or visual readability. |
| 5-20 min | WIP reviews. Two or three students/teams show work. |
| 20-26 min | Feedback round. Peers identify what worked, what was unclear, and one possible next improvement. |
| 26-29 min | Next-step commitments. Each presenting student/team states one improvement to test before the next review. |
| 29-30 min | Close. Teacher points students back to the relevant Google Classroom post or next task. |

### Review Protocol

For each presenter:

1. The creator gives a brief setup: what the work is and what feedback they want.
2. A peer interacts with it or responds to it as a user.
3. The creator observes first and explains later.
4. The peer says what they understood, felt, noticed, or found confusing.
5. The group gives short, respectful feedback.
6. The creator identifies one next design decision or test.

### Feedback Stems

- I understood that...
- I was confused by...
- I noticed...
- I wanted to...
- The strongest part is...
- One thing to test next is...

### Teacher Role

The teacher protects the time, keeps feedback respectful, and prevents the session from becoming a live debugging clinic.

Useful teacher phrases:

- "That is a useful technical problem, but today we are identifying the issue, not solving it live."
- "Put that in the Google Classroom Help Desk with a screenshot or error message."
- "Let us watch what the user does before we explain the design."
- "What is the next thing you can test before next Wednesday?"

### Output

By the end of the session, each presenting student or team should have:

- One piece of feedback they understand.
- One design or production problem to investigate.
- One next action to complete before the next check-in.

## Session Type 2: Orientation And Placement Meeting

### When To Use

Use this for the first meeting of a new intake or when a significant number of new students join.

### Focus

Introduce the studio culture, explain expectations, identify student experience levels, confirm laptop access, and place students into an appropriate starting pathway.

### What Students Bring

- A completed Google Form, if possible.
- A laptop if they have one.
- Any previous work they want to show.
- One project idea or area of interest.

### 30-Minute Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-5 min | Welcome and purpose of the studio. |
| 5-10 min | Explain expectations: production focus, respect, feedback, laptop access, Google Classroom use. |
| 10-18 min | Quick experience scan: tools used, projects made, laptop access, preferred interests. |
| 18-25 min | Introduce pathway: Explorer, Builder, Designer, Developer, Director. |
| 25-30 min | Assign next step: join Google Classroom, complete form, prepare first small task or demo. |

### Output

- Student interest confirmed.
- Laptop access clarified.
- Students loosely sorted into starting levels.
- First Google Classroom task assigned.

## Session Type 3: Showcase And Celebration

### When To Use

Use at the end of a short cycle, after a build challenge, before a holiday, or when students need motivation.

### Focus

Celebrate progress, let students demonstrate completed or improved work, and build community pride.

### What Students Bring

- A project or artifact that is ready to show.
- A short explanation of what they improved.
- One thing they learned.

### 30-Minute Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-3 min | Setup. |
| 3-5 min | Showcase rules and tone. |
| 5-23 min | Rapid demos. Each student/team gets two or three minutes. |
| 23-28 min | Audience appreciation and quick feedback cards. |
| 28-30 min | Close and preview the next cycle. |

### Output

- Students see each other's progress.
- Strong work is recognized.
- Students leave with motivation for the next cycle.

## Session Type 4: Critique By Design Lens

### When To Use

Use when many students are working on similar design problems, such as controls, UI, difficulty, feedback, story clarity, or visual readability.

### Focus

Review work through one specific design lens instead of giving general feedback.

### Example Lenses

- Are the controls clear?
- Does the player know what to do?
- Is the UI readable?
- Is the challenge fair?
- Does the game give useful feedback?
- Does the story make sense through play?
- Can a new user understand the screen without explanation?

### 30-Minute Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-5 min | Introduce the lens and one example. |
| 5-20 min | Review two or three works using only that lens. |
| 20-27 min | Group identifies patterns and common issues. |
| 27-30 min | Each team chooses one lens-based improvement. |

### Output

- Students learn to critique with focus.
- Feedback becomes more useful and less personal.
- Common issues can become future Google Classroom tutorials.

## Session Type 5: Planning And Team Formation

### When To Use

Use before a new project cycle, team build, showcase preparation period, or larger challenge.

### Focus

Help students form realistic teams, choose manageable project ideas, assign roles, and set short production goals.

### What Students Bring

- A project idea.
- Their preferred role or skill area.
- Laptop access information.
- A realistic sense of time.

### 30-Minute Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-5 min | Explain the challenge or project cycle. |
| 5-12 min | Students pitch quick ideas. |
| 12-20 min | Form teams around viable ideas and available laptops. |
| 20-27 min | Assign roles and first tasks. |
| 27-30 min | Record teams and next actions in Google Classroom. |

### Output

- Teams formed.
- Roles assigned.
- First build task posted or confirmed.

## Session Type 6: Problem Triage Meeting

### When To Use

Use when many students are stuck, but the session is still too short to solve problems live.

### Focus

Identify, categorize, and prioritize blockers so they can be handled later through Google Classroom, peer research, tutorials, or targeted support.

### Problem Categories

- Godot/editor issue.
- Code/script issue.
- Design decision.
- Art/asset issue.
- Team/project management issue.
- Laptop/access issue.
- Scope too large.

### 30-Minute Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-5 min | Students write blockers clearly. |
| 5-15 min | Teacher groups blockers by category. |
| 15-23 min | Identify common blockers that need a tutorial or resource. |
| 23-28 min | Assign research/helper tasks. |
| 28-30 min | Confirm where students should post evidence in Google Classroom. |

### Output

- Blockers are visible.
- Common problems become future posts/tutorials.
- Students know what evidence to submit for help.

## Google Classroom Support Structure

Recommended topics:

- Start Here
- Studio Announcements
- Core Lessons
- Tutorials
- Weekly Build Challenges
- Extension Quests
- WIP Submissions
- Help Desk Questions
- Showcase Submissions
- Resources

## Standing Studio Norms

1. Wednesday sessions are short and focused.
2. Students should come prepared with work, evidence, or a clear question.
3. The group follows the posted studio pathway.
4. Personal project questions should not hijack the session.
5. Technical issues are identified during Wednesday sessions but usually solved later.
6. Designers observe users before explaining their work.
7. Feedback should help the creator improve the next version.
8. Students without laptop access must be attached to a group that can demonstrate work.
